**Roles**
Warehouse records it. Planner handles what is left.

## The three delivery outcomes

| What happened | Odoo does | SCOP records |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Everything delivered | Closes the transfer | Stop **Completed**, demand **Completed** |
| Some delivered | Closes the transfer, **creates a backorder** | Stop **Partially Completed**, demand **Partially Completed**, follow-up demand created |
| Nothing delivered | — | Stop **Failed** with a reason, demand **Failed**, follow-up created |

All three are decided by the quantity on the validated transfer. Nobody chooses.

## A short delivery, step by step

| # | Who | Where | What |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Driver | At the door | Delivers what could be delivered |
| 2 | **Warehouse** | **Odoo** | Validates the transfer with **the quantity that actually moved** |
| 3 | Odoo | Odoo | Creates a **backorder** for the remainder |
| 4 | SCOP | — | Reads the actual quantity onto the stop line and the demand line |
| 5 | SCOP | — | Derives the outcome: **Partially Completed** |
| 6 | SCOP | — | No follow-up demand is created. **The Odoo backorder carries the remainder** on the same demand |
| 7 | Driver | The stop | Presses **Delivered**. `BR-EXE-001` asks for a reason |
| 8 | Planner | Next planning run | Plans the follow-up |


:::info[No follow-up demand is created — and a partial delivery does not need one]

There is not one record with **Is Follow-Up** set anywhere on `b2b`.

That costs nothing here: the Odoo **backorder** carries the remainder against the *same*
demand, and validating it completes the original. Verified end to end — 6 of 10, then the
remaining 4, then **Completed** across two transfers.

→ [Demand Completion](../demand/demand-completion.mdx) carries the finding in full.

## The backorder and the follow-up demand

Two things are created, in two different systems, and they are not the same thing.

| | Created by | Represents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Backorder** | **Odoo** | The remaining stock movement |
| **Follow-up demand** | **SCOP** | The remaining business requirement. **Never created** — the backorder does this job |

:::info[The original demand is not replaced]

`DMD-2026-000010` remains the customer's original requirement. It stays **Partially
Completed** until the remainder is delivered, and only then becomes **Completed**.

That is what keeps the promise measurable. OTIF asks whether the *original* requirement was
met — not whether the last backorder eventually arrived.

:::

### The demand chain

By design, on the follow-up demand's **Follow-Up and Returns** tab. These fields exist and
are displayed, but nothing populates them in Release 1:

| Field | Points to |
| --- | --- |
| **Parent Demand** | The demand this one follows up |
| **Is Follow-Up** | Set on this demand |
| **Root Demand** | The original, at the head of the chain |

A long chain — original, follow-up, follow-up of the follow-up — always names the same root.

## Why a backorder is not a new demand

:::info[A backorder is another fulfilment of the same requirement]

The link between a demand and Odoo is at **move level**, not at transfer level.

If a demand were tied to a single transfer, every partial delivery would appear as a
brand-new requirement, and the customer's original promise would be lost after the first
short delivery.

Because the link is at move level, one demand can be fulfilled by the original transfer plus
every backorder that follows it.

:::

This is why the **Transfers** count on a demand can be greater than one, and why that is
normal rather than a duplicate.

## Mixed outcomes at one stop

Several demands can share a physical visit. **They do not share a fate.**

| At one arrival | Its transfer | Demand outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `DMD-…010` | Validated in full | **Completed** |
| `DMD-…011` | Validated short, backorder created | **Partially Completed** |

The stop itself records **Partially Completed** — the visit did not do everything planned —
but each demand is resolved on its own transfer.

:::note[This is the point of keeping the demand link on every line]

A consolidated shipment saves a journey. It must not cost traceability, and it does not.

→ [The Physical Visit](../shipments/the-physical-visit.mdx)

:::

## The reason is required

`BR-EXE-001` — *a failed or partial stop must carry an outcome reason.*

The driver is asked at the moment they close the stop, because that is when they can
actually answer. The seven stop-outcome reasons are at
[Failed Delivery](./failed-delivery.mdx).

Without a reason, the stop cannot close — and neither can the trip.

## Completing the chain

By design the follow-up demand is a demand like any other. **In Release 1 it is never
created, so the remaining requirement has to be raised by hand:**

| Step | Where |
| --- | --- |
| It appears in the demand list | `Operations → Demands`, with **Is Follow-Up** set |
| It needs validating and marking eligible | The normal lifecycle |
| It forms its own shipment | For the remaining quantity |
| It gets planned | The next planning run |
| When it is delivered | The **original** demand becomes **Completed** |

:::warning[A follow-up demand does not plan itself]

It goes into the eligible pool like anything else. If nobody plans it, the original demand
sits at **Partially Completed** indefinitely.

Watch for follow-ups in the demand list, and check [Plan Coverage](../reporting/plan-coverage.mdx).

:::

## Effect on measurement

| Report | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| [OTIF](../reporting/otif.mdx) | **In Full** is false for a partial delivery. **On Time** is judged on arrival at the original stop |
| [Plan Coverage](../reporting/plan-coverage.mdx) | The follow-up is eligible demand that must be planned |
| [Trip Planned vs Actual](../reporting/trip-planned-vs-actual.mdx) | The trip closes as **Partially Completed** |

## Verify

| Check | Where | Expect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The transfer was validated short | Odoo | State **Done**, quantity less than ordered |
| A backorder exists | Odoo | A new transfer |
| The stop outcome | The stop | **Partially Completed** |
| The demand | `Operations → Demands` | **Partially Completed** |
| A follow-up demand exists | The demand's **Follow-Up and Returns** tab | **Empty in Release 1** — see the defect note above |
| The chain is intact | The follow-up's **Root Demand** | The original |
| The other demand at that stop | Its own status | Possibly **Completed** |

## If it does not work

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No backorder | Odoo was told not to create one | Check the Odoo transfer |
| No follow-up demand | **Expected in Release 1** — none is ever created | Raise the remainder by hand |
| The demand is **Completed** but was short | The transfer was validated with the **ordered** quantity | Correct it in Odoo |
| The follow-up is never planned | Nobody planned it | Include it in the next run |
| The original never reaches **Completed** | The follow-up has not been delivered | Deliver it |
| Both demands at one stop show the same state | Check each demand's own transfer — they may genuinely be the same |
