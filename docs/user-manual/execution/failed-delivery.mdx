**Role**
Driver records it. Operations acts on it.

## The distinction that matters most

:::danger[An unvalidated transfer is not a failed delivery]

These two look identical on screen — both show zero delivered — and they are completely
different things.

| Situation | What is true | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Odoo transfer has not been validated yet** | The goods may be at the customer. The paperwork is behind | **Wait.** Then press **Delivered** |
| **The delivery genuinely failed** | The goods came back on the vehicle | **Could not deliver**, with a reason |

:::

Getting this wrong is expensive: a failure recorded because the office had not caught up has
to be unpicked from OTIF, from the demand chain, and from every service measure afterwards.

### The two refusals, verbatim

Both reproduced on `b2b` as the driver:

```text
Stop 1 has no delivered quantity recorded yet. The warehouse validates the
transfer in Odoo, and SCOP reads the result — wait for that, then close the
stop. If the delivery genuinely failed, use 'Could not deliver' and give the
reason.
```

```text
Record why stop 2 failed before closing it.
```

The first offers you both paths. The second is `BR-EXE-001` refusing a failure with no
reason.

### How SCOP helps you tell them apart

When you press **Delivered** on a stop with no recorded quantity, SCOP does not simply
refuse. It says which case you are in:

> Stop 2 has no delivered quantity recorded yet. The warehouse validates the transfer in
> Odoo, and SCOP reads the result — wait for that, then close the stop. If the delivery
> genuinely failed, use 'Could not deliver' and give the reason.

## Could not deliver

**Navigate to** `SCOP → My Work → My Stops` → open the stop
**Available in** **Arrived** or **Servicing**

## The seven reasons

| Reason | Use when | Who should follow up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Customer location closed** | Nobody was there | Customer Service |
| **Customer refused the delivery** | They would not take it | Customer Service |
| **Goods damaged in transit** | It arrived unusable | Warehouse and Operations |
| **Access to the location blocked** | You could not reach the dock | Planning — the node may need constraints |
| **Ran out of time on the route** | The day ended first | Planning — the route was too long |
| **Vehicle issue** | The vehicle stopped you | Fleet |
| **Rescheduled at the customer's request** | They asked for another day | Customer Service |

:::note[Choose the reason that is true]

These are grouped and counted in [Exception Ageing](../reporting/exception-ageing.mdx) and in
operational review.

*Ran out of time on the route* appearing repeatedly is a planning signal somebody can act
on. Recording it as *Customer location closed* hides a fixable problem.

:::

## BR-EXE-001

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Code** | `BR-EXE-001` |
| **Name** | A failed or partial stop must carry an outcome reason |
| **Severity** | **Blocking** |
| **Subject** | Trip |
| **Fires at** | `trip_complete` |
| **If it cannot be evaluated** | **Block** |

It applies to **partial** deliveries as well as complete failures. Anything other than a
clean delivery says why.

:::info[Asked early, enforced late]

The rule is formally evaluated when the **trip** completes. But the driver is asked for the
reason when they close the **stop** — because that is the only moment somebody knows the
answer.

Waiting until trip completion to ask would mean a supervisor guessing on the driver's behalf
hours later.

:::

## Skipping a stop

Different from failing it.

| | Failed | Skipped |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Meaning** | The visit was attempted and achieved nothing | The visit was not attempted |
| **Available from** | Arrived, Servicing | Planned, Arrived |
| **Reason required** | Yes | Yes |

Skip a stop when the route was changed and you never went. Fail it when you went and it did
not work.


:::danger[Verified finding — a total failure leaves nothing behind]

There is **no backorder** behind a failed delivery, and **no follow-up demand is created** —
there is not one record with **Is Follow-Up** set anywhere on `b2b`. So nothing carries the
requirement forward and nobody is told.

`DMD-2026-000013` is **Failed**, terminal, with 5.0 still owed. Raise the replacement demand
by hand.

:::

## What happens afterwards

| Effect | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| The stop status becomes **Failed** | Terminal |
| The stop outcome becomes **Failed** | With the reason |
| The trip closes as **Partially Completed** | Not as Completed |
| The demand becomes **Failed** | If nothing at all was delivered |
| **A follow-up demand is created** | By design, so the requirement is not lost. **Not created in Release 1** |
| Loads and resources close normally | |

By design the follow-up demand goes into the eligible pool and must be planned like any
other. **In Release 1 it is never created**, so the second attempt has to be raised by hand.

## Raising an exception

A failed delivery does not automatically create an
[operational exception](../exceptions/exception-management.mdx). Exceptions are raised
deliberately, from the trip, when a problem needs owning and resolving.

| Failed delivery | Exception |
| --- | --- |
| A recorded outcome at one stop | A problem somebody must own |
| Always has a reason | Has a category, severity, owner and SLA |
| Closes with the stop | Has its own ten-state lifecycle |

Raise an exception when the failure needs follow-up beyond replanning the demand — a
customer dispute, damaged goods, a vehicle breakdown.

## Verify

| Check | Where | Expect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The stop failed with a reason | The stop | Status **Failed**, an **Outcome Reason** |
| The trip closed | The trip | **Partially Completed** |
| The demand | `Operations → Demands` | **Failed** |
| A follow-up exists | The demand's **Follow-Up and Returns** tab | **Empty in Release 1** |
| Photographs were captured | The **Proof of Delivery** tab | Where relevant |

## If it does not work

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Could not deliver** is refused | No reason chosen | Choose one |
| **Delivered** is refused, quantities zero | The transfer is not validated | **Wait.** Do not record a failure |
| The trip will not complete | A failed or partial stop has no reason — `BR-EXE-001` | Give it one |
| No follow-up demand | **Expected in Release 1** — none is ever created | Raise the remainder by hand |
| A failure was recorded by mistake | The stop is terminal and cannot be reopened | Raise the requirement again as a new demand |

:::warning[A closed stop cannot be reopened]

Failed, Skipped and Completed are all terminal. If a failure was recorded in error, the
correction is made through a **new demand for the remaining requirement**, not by
rewriting history. By design that is the follow-up demand; in Release 1 you raise it by hand.

That is deliberate: inventing transitions would put states in the audit trail that nobody
performed.

:::
