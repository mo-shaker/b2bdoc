## The situation

The driver arrives at **SCOP Manual Branch A** and the delivery cannot happen — the premises
are closed.

This scenario also covers the case that **looks identical on screen and is not a failure**.

## Setup

Follow [Standard Full Delivery](./standard-full-delivery.mdx) to the point where the trip has
departed and the driver has arrived at the customer stop.

---

## The fork in the road

At this point the driver's **Goods** tab shows **zero** delivered. Two completely different
things produce that.

| Situation | What is true |
| --- | --- |
| **The Odoo transfer has not been validated yet** | The goods may be at the customer. The paperwork is behind |
| **The delivery genuinely failed** | The goods came back on the vehicle |

:::danger[Getting this wrong is expensive]

A failure recorded because the office had not caught up has to be unpicked from OTIF, from
the demand chain, and from every service measure afterwards.

:::

### How to tell them apart

Ask one question: **did the goods come off the vehicle?**

| Answer | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Yes, the customer has them | **Wait** for validation, then **Delivered** |
| No, they are still on board | **Could not deliver**, with a reason |

### How SCOP helps

Pressing **Delivered** with no recorded quantity does not simply refuse:

> Stop 2 has no delivered quantity recorded yet. The warehouse validates the transfer in
> Odoo, and SCOP reads the result — wait for that, then close the stop. If the delivery
> genuinely failed, use 'Could not deliver' and give the reason.

The message names both paths on purpose.

---

## Step 1 — Record the failure

**Role** Driver
**Where** `SCOP → My Work → My Stops` → open the stop

Press **Could not deliver**.

:::danger[A reason is required]

`BR-EXE-001` will not let the stop close without one. You are asked at the door, because that
is when you can answer.

:::

Choose **Customer location closed**.

### The seven reasons

| Reason | Follow-up belongs to |
| --- | --- |
| **Customer location closed** | Customer Service |
| **Customer refused the delivery** | Customer Service |
| **Goods damaged in transit** | Warehouse and Operations |
| **Access to the location blocked** | Planning — the node may need constraints |
| **Ran out of time on the route** | Planning — the route was too long |
| **Vehicle issue** | Fleet |
| **Rescheduled at the customer's request** | Customer Service |

:::note[Choose the reason that is true]

*Ran out of time on the route* recorded honestly is a planning signal somebody can act on.
Recorded as *Customer location closed*, it hides a fixable problem.

:::

---

## Step 2 — Photograph the evidence

**Where** The **Proof of delivery** tab

Attach a photograph of the closed premises. Photographs resolve most delivery disputes and
cost a moment.

---

## Step 3 — What SCOP records

| Record | Becomes |
| --- | --- |
| The stop | Status **Failed**, outcome **Failed**, with the reason |
| The trip | **Partially Completed** when it closes |
| The demand | **Failed** |
| **A follow-up demand** | Created — the requirement is not lost |
| Loads and resources | Close normally |

**Verify**

| Check | Where | Expect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The stop failed with a reason | The stop | Status and **Outcome Reason** |
| The trip closed | The trip | **Partially Completed** |
| The demand | `Operations → Demands` | **Failed** |
| A follow-up exists | The demand's **Follow-Up and Returns** tab | A child demand |
| The evidence | The **Proof of Delivery** tab | The photograph |

---

## Step 4 — Decide what happens next

**Role** Operations Manager

A failed delivery does **not** automatically create an operational exception. Raise one when
the failure needs owning beyond replanning.

| Raise an exception when | Do not when |
| --- | --- |
| Goods were damaged | The customer was simply closed |
| The customer disputes it | It will just be redelivered |
| A vehicle failed | — |
| It is the third failure this week at one branch | — |

→ [Exception Management](../exceptions/exception-management.mdx)

**Role** Planner

Plan the follow-up demand for another day.

## The variation — late paperwork, not a failure

Repeat the setup, but this time the goods **were** delivered and the warehouse simply has not
validated the transfer.

| # | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | The driver presses **Delivered** |
| 2 | SCOP refuses, and explains that no quantity has been recorded yet |
| 3 | **The driver waits.** They do **not** press Could not deliver |
| 4 | The warehouse validates the transfer in Odoo |
| 5 | The driver presses **Delivered** again — it succeeds |
| 6 | The demand reaches **Completed** |

:::info[The two cases end completely differently]

| | Genuine failure | Late paperwork |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Demand | **Failed** | **Completed** |
| Trip | Partially Completed | Completed |
| Follow-up demand | Created | None |
| OTIF | Not in full, not on time | On time, in full |

One driver decision, made in ten seconds at a customer's door, decides all of that.

:::

---

## What this proves

| Property | Demonstrated by |
| --- | --- |
| `BR-EXE-001` requires a reason | Step 1 |
| The reason is asked at the moment somebody can answer | Step 1 |
| A failure does not lose the requirement | Step 3 — the follow-up |
| SCOP distinguishes a failure from missing paperwork | The variation |
| A closed stop cannot be reopened | Below |

:::warning[A closed stop is terminal]

Failed, Skipped and Completed cannot be reopened. If a failure was recorded in error, the
correction is made through the **follow-up demand**, not by rewriting history.

Inventing transitions would put states in the audit trail that nobody performed.

:::
