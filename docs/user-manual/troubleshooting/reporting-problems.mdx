:::warning[Most reporting problems are not report problems]

They are data problems that the report is faithfully showing.

Before investigating a figure, check the three data-quality reports — see
[Data Quality Reports](../reporting/data-quality-reports.mdx).

:::

## A delivery is missing from OTIF

### 1 · It carries no commitment

**How to verify** Clear the default filter and group by **Has Commitment**.
**Cause** OTIF opens filtered to records that have a commitment. Nothing was promised, so
nothing can be measured.
**Correct action** None, unless a commitment should have existed. See
[Service Commitments](../configuration/service-commitments.mdx).

### 2 · The demand has not finished

**How to verify** The demand's state.
**Cause** Only **Completed**, **Partially Completed** and **Failed** demands appear.
**Correct action** Finish it — usually by validating the Odoo transfer.

### 3 · It predates go-live

**Cause** There is no historical backfill.
**Correct action** None.

## OTIF looks too good

### 1 · The commitment filter is hiding most deliveries

**How to verify** Clear the filter and count how many rows have **Has Commitment** true
against false.
**Cause** If only a small proportion carry commitments, the measure is thin and unrepresentative.
**Correct action** Record commitments where they exist. Report the **count** alongside the
percentage.

:::danger[A rising OTIF with a falling commitment count is not an improvement]

Fewer measured deliveries can mean the remaining ones are simply the easy ones.

:::

### 2 · Transfers are validated with the ordered quantity

**How to verify** Compare a sample of validated transfers against what was actually delivered.
**Cause** Validating the ordered quantity when less moved produces a Completed demand, a clean
**In Full**, and a customer who did not get their goods.
**Correct action** Validate what actually moved. This is a training matter.

### 3 · Node timezones are wrong

**How to verify** Compare **Arrived (local)** on a row against the window, and check the
node's timezone.
**Cause** A wrong timezone shifts every arrival at that node by the offset.
**Correct action** Correct the timezone. Note that history before the correction is not
comparable with history after.

## Plan Coverage looks too good

### 1 · Orphan moves exist

**How to verify** `Reporting → Orphan Moves` — is it empty?
**Cause** Stock is moving on demand-generating transfers that **no demand line covers**. That
demand was never offered, so it never counts against coverage.

:::danger[Coverage is overstated by exactly this much]

:::

**Correct action** Re-save the transfers. If that does not fix it, check Failed Projections.

### 2 · Demand never became eligible

**How to verify** `Operations → Demands`, grouped by **Status**. Look at the counts in
**Submitted** and **Validated**.
**Cause** Coverage measures against **eligible** demand. Requirements stuck earlier are
invisible to it.
**Correct action** Work the demand queue. Read coverage alongside the status distribution.

### 3 · Coverage percentages are being summed

**Cause** Coverage is a percentage and must be **averaged**.
**Correct action** For a period figure, sum **Offered** and **Planned** and divide.

## A report is empty

| Cause | Verify | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A default filter | Clear the search bar | — |
| No data since go-live | The pilot go-live date | Expected early in a pilot |
| Nothing has finished yet | The relevant records' states | Reports mostly cover finished work |
| Nobody raises exceptions | The exception list | See below |

:::warning[An empty exception queue is not automatically good news]

Exceptions are raised **deliberately**. A queue that is always empty in a busy operation
usually means problems are handled informally and never recorded — so they cannot be counted,
escalated or fixed at source.

:::

---

## An exception has no SLA deadline

**How to verify** The **SLA Deadline** field is empty.
**Cause** Its **category** has no SLA hours configured. The deadline is computed from
**Detected At** plus the category's SLA the moment the exception is created, so an empty
deadline points at the category, not at the exception.
**Correct action** Set SLA hours on the category at `Governance → Exception Categories`.

:::note[Being unclassified does not stop an exception ageing]

A category is required to create an exception at all, so even one still sitting in
**Detected** has a deadline and will show as overdue once it passes.

:::

---

## Exception Ageing seems to prioritise wrongly

**Cause** You are sorting by age rather than by age **against the category's SLA**.
**Correct action** Filter to **Overdue**, then sort by **Severity**. A three-hour vehicle
breakdown (2 h SLA) is more urgent than a thirty-hour supplier delay (24 h SLA).

## Planned and actual diverge on every trip

**How to verify** `Reporting → Trip Planned vs Actual` — is the variance consistent across
vehicles and drivers?
**Cause** A consistent gap is an **estimate** problem, not a performance problem.

| Pattern | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Duration consistently over | Service durations on the nodes are too low |
| Distance consistently over | The travel matrix distances are short |
| One vehicle only | Its average speed is wrong |
| One node only | Its service duration is wrong |
| Cost only | The cost rates are wrong |

**Correct action** Fix the master data. Every future plan improves, and OTIF improves with it.

:::warning[Do not read a fleet-wide pattern as driver performance]

:::

---

## Decision Quality shows a high override rate

**Cause** Usually the recommendation is wrong in a specific, nameable way.
**How to verify** Group by **Override Reason**.

| Dominant reason | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| *Incorrect master data* | **Fix the data.** These modifications disappear |
| *Local route knowledge* | The travel matrix is wrong |
| *Operational information unavailable to the engine* | The system lacks an input — a roadmap item |
| Varied reasons | Ordinary operational judgement. Nothing to fix |

:::note[Do not target a low override rate]

It would be achieved by accepting bad plans. A planner overriding a poor recommendation is
doing their job.

:::

---

## Cost figures do not match the accounts

**Cause** Cost rates are **operational estimates for comparing plans**. Nothing reconciles
them to the ledger.
**Correct action** None. Do not quote them as a cost of sale.

→ [Release 1 Scope](../about/release-1-scope.mdx)

## Ownership Data Quality shows a large unresolved population

**How to verify** Group by **Customer**, then by **Demand Type**.

| Pattern | Cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One customer | A missing consignment flag | `Onboarding → 1 · Consignment Partners` |
| Inbound only | Consignment partners not flagged | Same |
| Outbound, spread widely | The warehouse is not recording owners on reserved stock | An Odoo process change |
| Nothing resolved at all | Migration was never run | `Onboarding → 2 · Inventory Ownership` |

:::info[Fix the pattern, not the demands]

A hundred unresolved lines caused by one unflagged partner is one fix, not a hundred.

:::

---

## Failed Projections has entries

**Cause** SCOP tried to record something and failed. Generation is non-blocking, so the
transfer saved anyway and nothing complained.
**Correct action** Read the error message, fix the cause, re-save the transfer.

During a pilot this screen deserves a daily glance.
