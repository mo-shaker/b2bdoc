## Purpose

SCOP uses standard Odoo screens. This page covers only the patterns that **change how you
read a SCOP record** — it is not a general Odoo tutorial.

## The status bar is the lifecycle

Every operational SCOP record shows its lifecycle across the top of the form. The stage you
are in is highlighted; the stages before and after it are shown so you can see where the
record is going.

```text
Draft → Submitted → Validated → Eligible for Planning → Planned → Released → In Execution → Completed
```

Two things about it are worth knowing.

**The status bar hides the unhappy paths.** A demand can also be **Rejected**, **Cancelled**
or **Failed**, but those are not on the bar because they are not steps on the way anywhere.
The record still shows them once it reaches them.

**You cannot click the status bar to change state.** SCOP lifecycles move only through the
buttons in the header, because each button runs the checks that belong to that transition.

:::info[Why a state cannot be set directly]

Writing a state by hand would skip the rule evaluation that goes with it — a plan could be
approved without checking who approved it, or a demand validated without checking who owns
the goods. SCOP refuses a direct write to a governed state and tells you to use the
record's own button.

:::

## Buttons appear and disappear

The header only offers the actions that are legal **right now**. A demand in **Submitted**
offers **Validate** and **Reject**; the same demand in **Validated** offers **Mark
Eligible** instead.

If a button you expect is not there, the usual reason is that the record is not in the state
that offers it. The second most common reason is that your role is not entitled to it — for
example **Stage Everything** appears only for Warehouse users.

## Colour and badges in lists

SCOP colours list rows to surface problems without opening anything.

| What you see | Meaning | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Red row** | Ownership is unresolved and the demand is past Draft | Demands list |
| **Grey row** | Cancelled or rejected | Demands list |
| **Green badge** | Completed | Demands, shipments |
| **Amber badge** | Partially completed | Demands |
| **Red badge** | Failed | Demands |

An **Ownership** column is shown in the demands list on purpose: it tells a planner that a
demand will refuse to validate *before* they press the button and find out. See
[Inventory Ownership](../ownership/inventory-ownership.mdx).

## Kanban boards show the whole lifecycle

Demands, trips and stops offer a card view grouped by status.

SCOP deliberately shows **every lifecycle column, including the empty ones**, in lifecycle
order rather than alphabetical order. An operator reading the board needs to see that
nothing is in **Exception** just as much as they need to see what is.

## The chatter records who did what

At the foot of every operational form is the message and activity panel.

State changes are **tracked**: each transition writes a line saying who moved the record,
when, and from what to what. That log is part of the audit trail and is not editable.

Use it when you need to answer *"who approved this, and when?"* without leaving the record.
For a wider reconstruction across several records, use
[Operation Timeline](../reporting/operation-timeline.mdx) instead.

## Reports are pivot tables

Most SCOP reports open as a list, with a pivot view available. The pivot is where the
report earns its value: drag a dimension onto rows, a second onto columns, and choose a
measure.

Three conventions apply across every SCOP report:

| Convention | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Reports are **read-only** | You cannot fix data from a report. Open the underlying record |
| Some reports open with a **default filter** | For example OTIF opens filtered to records that carry a commitment. Clear it deliberately, not accidentally |
| A percentage measure is **averaged**, not summed | Summing coverage percentages across five days produces a meaningless number |

Each report's own page under [Reporting](../reporting/reporting-overview.mdx) names its
dimensions and measures explicitly.

## Stat buttons open the related work

Several forms carry buttons in the top-right that show a count and open the records behind
it — for example **Transfers** on a demand, which opens the Odoo transfers fulfilling it.

These are the fastest way to cross from SCOP into Odoo and back.

## What you cannot edit

A large part of a SCOP record is **computed**, not typed. Readiness, staged percentage,
fulfilled quantity, capacity feasibility, planned versus actual variance — all are derived
from evidence elsewhere.

If a computed field looks wrong, the fix is never to change the field. It is to correct the
evidence and let SCOP recompute. Each chapter names the evidence for the values it covers.
