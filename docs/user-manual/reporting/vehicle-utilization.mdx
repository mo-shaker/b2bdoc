## Purpose

Answers **how full the vehicles were** — not on average over a trip, but **at every point of
every route**.

## Audience

Planner / Dispatcher and Management.

## Navigate to

`SCOP → Reporting → Vehicle Utilization`

An Administrator can read the same underlying rows at
`SCOP → Technical → Capacity Snapshots`.

## Filters

Opens **grouped by vehicle**. Filter by date range or trip.

## Dimensions

| Dimension | Use |
| --- | --- |
| **Vehicle** | The default row dimension |
| **Trip** | One route at a time |
| **Stop** | The point on the route |
| **Sequence** | Position in the route |

## Measures

| Measure | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Weight %** | How full by weight at this point |
| **Volume %** | How full by volume at this point |

The underlying rows also carry **Used Weight**, **Remaining Weight**, **Used Volume**,
**Remaining Volume**, **Feasible**, **Limiting Dimension** and **Breached Dimensions**.

:::info[There is one row per point of the route, not one per trip]

A mixed pickup-and-delivery trip changes load after each stop. A vehicle that leaves the
depot at 80% may be at 110% after a collection in the middle.

Measuring only at departure would miss exactly the case that matters.

:::

## How to read it

The figure that matters operationally is the **peak** — the fullest the vehicle gets at any
point. The trip form carries it directly as **Peak Weight %** and **Peak Volume %**.

| Peak | Reading |
| --- | --- |
| **Above 100%** | Infeasible. `BR-RTE-001` blocks it |
| **85–100%** | Well used |
| **60–85%** | Reasonable |
| **30–60%** | Under-used — sometimes correct, sometimes not |
| **Below 30%** | Something is wrong, or the day was genuinely light |

### Weight against volume

Compare the two. The **limiting dimension** tells you what kind of fleet problem you have.

| Pattern | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Weight high, volume low | Dense goods. You need payload, not space |
| Volume high, weight low | Bulky goods. You need space, not payload |
| Both moderate | Well matched |
| Both low | Too much vehicle for the work, or not enough work |

## Example

```text
Vehicle              Peak Weight %   Peak Volume %
SCOP Manual Vehicle       92%             41%
Van 2                     34%             38%
Van 3                     88%             35%
```

Two readings:

| Observation | Reading |
| --- | --- |
| Weight consistently far above volume | The fleet is volume-rich and payload-poor for these goods |
| Van 2 at a third on both | Either it should not have gone out, or its work should have been consolidated |

## Common interpretation mistakes

:::warning[A low figure is not automatically waste]

Utilisation is **rank 7** in the objective profile. Customer commitments are **rank 3**.

SCOP will send a half-empty vehicle rather than break a promise, and that is the ranking
working correctly.

→ [Planning Overview](../planning/planning-overview.mdx)

:::

| Mistake | Why it is wrong |
| --- | --- |
| Averaging across every point of a route | The **peak** is what constrains the vehicle, not the mean |
| Averaging across trips | A full trip and an empty one do not average to a meaningful figure |
| Treating a `BR-RTE-002` warning as an error | It is a warning by design |
| Reading it without the limiting dimension | You cannot tell whether you need payload or space |
| Concluding the fleet is too big from one week | Seasonality is real |

## What to do about a persistent finding

| Finding | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Consistently low peaks across the fleet | Consider smaller vehicles, or consolidating more work per trip |
| One vehicle always low | Check whether its capacity profile is wrong, or its availability is limiting it |
| Weight always the limiting dimension | The fleet's payload is the constraint |
| Frequent `BR-RTE-001` breaches | Vehicles are too small for the loads being planned |
