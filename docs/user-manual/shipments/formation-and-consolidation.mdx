**Role**
Nobody. SCOP does this.

## The rule in one line

:::warning[One Demand ≠ always one Shipment]

A shipment is a **transport requirement**, not a copy of a demand. Two compatible demands
form **one** shipment; two incompatible ones form two.

:::

## When formation happens

The moment a demand reaches **Eligible for Planning**.

```text
Validated Demand  →  Allocate Inventory  →  Create Shipment
                     └── both done by Mark Eligible ──┘
```

Nobody presses a formation button. **Mark Eligible** refreshes allocation and forms the
shipment in the same action, because that is the point in the flow where availability is
asserted.

:::info[Why grouping is decided here and not at demand creation]

Grouping is a **planning** judgement. Two demands to one branch may travel together tomorrow
and separately the day after, depending on capacity and dates.

Forming a shipment per demand at generation time would fix that choice before anyone had the
information to make it.

:::

## The consolidation key

Two demands form one shipment when **all five** of these match:

| # | Must match | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Origin node** | A vehicle loads at one place |
| 2 | **Destination node** | A different branch is a different place |
| 3 | **Shipment type** | A pickup is not a delivery |
| 4 | **Company** | Records never cross companies |
| 5 | **Required date** | Next Thursday's requirement must not be folded into today's |

If any differ, two shipments are formed.

:::note[Inbound demands become collections]

A receipt SCOP transports is a **collection** from the supplier's node. There is no separate
inbound shipment type, because calling it one would put it on the wrong side of every
direction-aware rule.

:::

## Joining an open shipment

When a second demand becomes eligible and matches the key, it **joins the shipment already
open** rather than forming another.

**Open** means both of:

| Condition | Why |
| --- | --- |
| The shipment is still **Created** or **Ready** | |
| It is **not yet on a trip** | Its capacity was sized when the plan was approved |

:::warning[A planned shipment is never joined]

Once a shipment is on a trip, a planner has already sized a vehicle against its weight and
volume. Quietly adding a pallet to it would break the capacity figure the plan was approved
on.

A later demand for the same route and date forms a **second** shipment instead, which the
next planning run can place.

:::

## Why consolidation is done at the moment, not later

Demand becomes eligible one record at a time. Forming a fresh shipment for each would mean
the daily plan never sees two requirements travel together — the exact opposite of what
consolidation exists for.

Joining at the moment the second requirement appears is what makes it happen without anyone
having to notice the opportunity.

## Formation is idempotent

A demand that already has shipment lines is **already formed**. Running formation again
returns its existing shipment rather than creating a second.

That is what makes **Create Shipment** safe to press.

## Create Shipment — the re-run

**Create Shipment** appears on a demand in **Eligible for Planning**.

| Use it for | Not for |
| --- | --- |
| A demand made eligible before automatic formation shipped | Normal operation |
| A demand whose shipment was cancelled | Trying to force a separate shipment |

Pressing it on a normal demand **returns the shipment that already exists**. It never
creates a duplicate.

It is refused on a demand that is not yet eligible: a shipment built from an unvalidated
demand would carry ownership nobody has checked onto a vehicle.

## What the shipment inherits

| From the demand line | Onto the shipment line |
| --- | --- |
| Product | Product |
| **Remaining** quantity, or requested if none | Quantity |
| Owner | Owner |
| Ownership source | Ownership source |
| The demand itself | **Demand** — the traceability link |

Using **remaining** rather than requested is what would make a follow-up demand form a shipment
for the outstanding part only.

## After formation

SCOP immediately:

1. Attaches the relevant Odoo transfers to the shipment.
2. Creates the **Warehouse Readiness** record.
3. Computes readiness for the first time.

**Expected result:** a shipment in state **Created** or **Ready**, usually with readiness
**Conditionally Ready** — reserved for the whole shipment but not yet staged.

## Verify consolidation worked

| Check | Expect |
| --- | --- |
| Two eligible demands, same route and date | **One** shipment |
| Its total weight | The sum of both demands |
| Its **Lines** tab | Lines naming **both** demands |
| The resulting trip | **One** customer delivery stop, not two |

The last one is the point of it all — see [The Physical Visit](./the-physical-visit.mdx).

## If consolidation did not happen

| Symptom | Cause |
| --- | --- |
| Two shipments for the same customer | Different **branch** — a different node is a different destination |
| Two shipments for the same node | Different **required date** |
| Two shipments, same node and date | Different **shipment type** — one is a pickup |
| A late demand formed its own shipment | The first shipment was already **planned** onto a trip |
| Consolidation happened when you did not want it | It is automatic. Change the required date, or plan them separately |

## If formation did not happen at all

| Check | Where |
| --- | --- |
| Is the demand actually **Eligible for Planning**? | The demand's status bar |
| Does it have lines? | The **Lines** tab. A demand with no lines forms no shipment |
| Did **Mark Eligible** complete? | The chatter on the demand |

:::note[Formation failing means eligibility fails too]

Formation is deliberately not wrapped in something that swallows failures. A demand that
reached **Eligible for Planning** with no shipment is precisely the state a pilot cannot
plan around — the warehouse has nothing to stage against and the planner has nothing to put
on a vehicle.

If formation cannot succeed, the demand does not become eligible either.

:::
