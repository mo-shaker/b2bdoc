## What a Demand is

A **Demand** is one business requirement to move goods: *this much of this product, from
here to there, by this date, owned by this party.*

It is deliberately separate from the paperwork behind it. The customer order is Odoo's
record of a commercial agreement; the delivery order is Odoo's record of a warehouse
transaction. The **demand** is SCOP's record of *what must move*, and it survives both.

## Why it is not the same as a transfer

There is **no one-to-one link** between a demand and an Odoo transfer.

```text
One transfer  →  may carry several demands
One demand    →  may be fulfilled by several transfers
                 (the original, plus every backorder)
```

The authoritative link is at **move level**. That is what makes a backorder attach itself
to the demand it came from without anyone re-keying anything:

:::info[A backorder is another fulfilment of the same demand, not a new one]

If a demand were tied to a single transfer, every partial delivery would appear as a
brand-new requirement, and the customer's original promise would be lost after the first
short delivery.

Because the link is at move level, `DMD-2026-000010` stays one requirement whether it
takes one delivery or four.

:::

## Where a demand comes from

You do not create one. A demand is generated **automatically** when Odoo creates a transfer
whose operation type is inside the pilot scope.

→ [How Demands Are Created](./how-demands-are-created.mdx)

## The Demands list

`SCOP → Operations → Demands`

<Screenshot
  src="quick-start/03-scop-demand-list.png"
  alt="SCOP Demands list showing several demands with their required date, customer, destination, quantities, ownership flag and status badge."
  caption="One row per demand. The Ownership column tells a planner which demands will refuse to validate — before they press the button and find out."
/>

| Column | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Reference** | The business identifier, `DMD-2026-000010` |
| **Required Date** | When the goods must be there |
| **Requesting Party** | The party the movement is for |
| **Destination Location** | The planning node it goes to |
| **Demand Type** | Delivery · Collection · Transfer · Return · Inbound |
| **Priority** | Low · Normal · High · Urgent |
| **Requested / Fulfilled / Remaining** | Quantities, with column totals |
| **Ownership** | Whether the owner is resolved on every line |
| **Status** | The lifecycle state, as a badge |

Two optional columns can be switched on: **Origin** (the node it comes from) and **Origin
Type** (where the requirement came from).

### Reading the colours

| What you see | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Red row** | Ownership unresolved, and the demand is past Draft. It cannot validate |
| **Grey row** | Cancelled or Rejected |
| **Green badge** | Completed |
| **Amber badge** | Partially Completed |
| **Red badge** | Failed |

**Validated** is work waiting for someone.

## The Demand form

<Screenshot

  alt="SCOP Demand form showing the status bar, requirement and routing fields, and the Lines tab with product, quantity and owner."
  caption="The demand form. The status bar across the top is the lifecycle; the buttons beside it are the only way to move along it."

### The header

The status bar shows the lifecycle:

```text
Draft → Submitted → Validated → Eligible for Planning → Planned → Released → In Execution → Completed
```

**Rejected**, **Cancelled** and **Failed** are not on the bar because they are not steps on
the way anywhere. The record still shows them once it reaches one.

The buttons beside it offer only what is legal now — see
[Demand Lifecycle](./demand-lifecycle.mdx).

### The ownership banner

If ownership is unresolved on any line, a warning appears at the **top** of the form, not
buried in a tab:

> Ownership is unresolved on at least one line. This demand stays visible and editable, but
> it cannot pass validation until an owner is recorded — it is never defaulted to the
> company.

That placement is deliberate: an unresolved owner is the single most common reason a demand
cannot move, and it is a legal problem rather than a tidiness one.

→ [Inventory Ownership](../ownership/inventory-ownership.mdx)

### Requirement

| Field | Meaning | Editable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Requesting Party** | The party the movement is for | In Draft |
| **Demand Type** | The kind of movement | In Draft |
| **Origin Type** | Where the requirement came from | In Draft |
| **Source Document** | The captured number of the order behind it, e.g. `S09520` | No — captured at generation |
| **Source Document Link** | The technical reference to that record | Administrator only |
| **Priority** | Low · Normal · High · Urgent | Yes |

:::note[Source Document is a captured label, not a live link]

The demand stores the source order's **number** when it is generated. Reading it needs no
rights over the order — a planner sees `S09520` without holding any Sales permission, and
gains none.

The live **Source Document Link** is shown only to an Administrator, because rendering it
makes the browser resolve the target record under the reader's own rights.

:::

### Route and Timing

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Origin** | The planning node the goods leave from |
| **Destination** | The planning node they go to |
| **Required Date** | When they must be there |
| **Service Commitment** | What was promised to this party, if anything |

Both endpoints must resolve to a [planning node](../configuration/planning-nodes.mdx), or
the demand is never created in the first place.

### The Transfers button

The stat button at the top right shows how many Odoo transfers are fulfilling this demand,
and opens them. It is the fastest way to cross from SCOP into Odoo.

A count above one is normal and expected — it means backorders.

## Demand lines

The **Lines** tab is where the substance is. One line per product.

| Field | Meaning | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Product** | What must move | The Odoo move |
| **Unit of Measure** | How it is counted | The product |
| **Requested** | How much was asked for | The Odoo move |
| **Fulfilled** | How much has actually moved | **Read from validated Odoo transfers** |
| **Remaining** | Requested minus fulfilled | Computed |
| **Owner** | Who owns these goods | **Derived**, never defaulted |
| **Ownership Source** | How the owner was established | Derived |
| **Lot** and **Expiry** | Where tracked | Optional columns |

:::warning[Fulfilled quantity is never typed]

**Fulfilled** is read back from validated Odoo transfers. There is no place in SCOP where
anyone enters how much was delivered.

→ [Odoo Delivery Validation](../execution/odoo-delivery-validation.mdx)

:::

Ownership is resolved **per line**, not per demand, because one requirement can legitimately
mix company-owned and customer-owned goods.

## The other tabs

| Tab | Shows | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Allocations** | The specific stock reserved — location, lot, owner, quantity | Read-only. Refreshed on Validate and Mark Eligible |
| **Shipments** | The shipment or shipments carrying this demand | A consolidated shipment may carry several demands |
| **Follow-Up and Returns** | The demand chain, the return sub-cycle, and outcome reasons | See [Demand Completion](./demand-completion.mdx) |
| **Technical** | Correlation reference, company, archive flag | Administrator only |

### The Follow-Up and Returns tab

| Group | Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chain | **Parent Demand** | The demand this one follows up |
| Chain | **Is Follow-Up** | Whether this demand exists because an earlier one was short |
| Chain | **Root Demand** | The original requirement at the head of the chain |
| Chain | **Split From** | Where the demand was split rather than followed up |
| Return | **Return Status**, **Return Reason**, **Disposition** | Only for return demands |
| Outcome | **Rejection Reason**, **Unplanned Reason**, **Next Action** | Why it was rejected, or not planned |

## Who can do what

| Action | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Read a demand | Any SCOP role except Driver |
| Validate, Mark Eligible, Resolve Ownership, Reject, Cancel, Replan | **Planner** |
| Delete a demand | **Administrator** |
| See it at all | **Not a Driver** — see [Driver Security Model](../execution/driver-security-model.mdx) |
