## Why this page exists

A guide that describes capability the system does not have wastes a tester's afternoon and damages trust in everything else it says. Everything below was re-checked against the `b2b` database at `http://localhost:1701/` for this revision.

## Not in Release 1

These are sometimes assumed to be present. They are not.

| Not available | What Release 1 does instead |
| --- | --- |
| **Live vehicle tracking** | Location is captured **once**, at the point of delivery, as part of proof of delivery. SCOP does not follow the vehicle between stops |
| **Real-time telematics** | No telematics integration exists. Vehicle data is master data plus what the driver records |
| **AI route optimisation** | Two decision providers ship: a **deterministic baseline** recommender and a **manual** provider that validates a person's own assignments. Neither is an AI optimiser |
| **Accounting reconciliation** | Cost rates on vehicles are operational estimates for planning comparisons, explicitly not accounting figures. No reconciliation to the ledger |
| **Historical backfill** | There is deliberately **no demand backfill**. SCOP starts at the pilot go-live cut-off. A reconstructed planning history would be fiction presented as evidence |
| **Purchase-order consignment terms** | Not modelled. Consignment ownership comes from a flagged partner, not from order terms |

:::info[Prediction and forecasting]

The wider SCOP vision includes predicted travel times, arrival estimates and risk detection. Those belong to the AI Platform roadmap, not to Release 1. What ships today is service durations and travel estimates from master data, and a deterministic recommendation.

:::

## Prerequisites the system does not enforce

| Requirement | Consequence if missed |
| --- | --- |
| Odoo **Fleet → Administrator** on planners | The planning wizard raises `Access Error` on `Vehicle`. Odoo's own *Fleet → Officer* is not enough: it carries a record rule narrowing the user to the vehicles they personally drive |
| Odoo **Employees → Officer** on planners | The **Driver** field on a trip never finishes loading its options, so no driver can be assigned — and a trip without a driver never appears in anyone's **My Trips** |
| A pilot-scope record, applied | No demand is created at all |
| A SCOP planning node for every destination | Deliveries to that destination produce no demand. The reason is recorded — see below |
| Coordinates, timezone and opening hours on each node | Demand becomes unplannable with *missing required data* |
| An `https` deployment | Delivery coordinates record as null with a reason |

:::note[Reading a SCOP screen needs no Odoo rights; operating a demand needs Inventory]

Delivered quantities are Odoo's record, and SCOP only mirrors them. The Demand form and the driver's stop screen now read those quantities on the record's behalf, so an operational SCOP user no longer needs Odoo's **Inventory → User** group simply to open a SCOP screen — and a driver, who must never be able to read every transfer in the company, does not need it at all.

A planner still needs **Inventory → User** to *operate* a demand — resolving ownership and marking it eligible refresh allocation against Odoo's reservations — and whoever validates a transfer needs it because that is Odoo's own work.

No Sales permission is required at any point. The demand carries the source order's number, captured when it was generated, so the planner reads **Source Document** without access to the order itself.

:::

## When a delivery does not appear in SCOP

A transfer whose origin or destination resolves to no SCOP planning node produces no demand. That is deliberate — a demand nobody can route or measure is worse than no demand — but it is **no longer silent**.

Each skip is recorded as a queryable event under `SCOP → Technical → Domain Events`, with the event name `DemandGenerationSkipped`. The row names the transfer, the unmapped stock location, the partner, and which end of the movement could not be resolved.

Map the location to a SCOP planning node and re-save the transfer; the demand is then created normally. The skip marker is kept apart from the generation marker precisely so that it never reads as *"already handled"*.

## Known display issue

| Item | Effect | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Staged % renders a hundred times too large** | `SHP-…` rows on `SCOP → Operations → Shipments`, and the same field on the shipment form, show `10000%` where they mean `100%`. The stored value is correct — only the column is scaled twice | Read it as a hundredth, or use **Readiness** instead, which is computed from the same evidence and is not affected. Plan Coverage is unaffected — it was corrected earlier |

This is cosmetic: readiness, planning and every measure downstream use the underlying value, which is right.

## Defects found during UAT, now fixed

Earlier Quick Start revisions documented defects discovered during UAT, together with the workarounds they required. **All of them have been remediated and covered by automated tests, and re-verified on `b2b` for this revision.** The workarounds are gone, and so are the instructions that recommended them. The table is kept as a record of what changed, not as a list of things to expect:

| Fixed | What it means now |
| --- | --- |
| **Shipment formation** | Marking a demand **Eligible for Planning** forms its shipment automatically. **Create Shipment** remains on the form only as a re-run for an exceptional case. No administrator involved |
| **Plan approval and release** | The seeded approval authority is now consulted, so an **Operations Manager** approves and releases. `BR-PLN-001` still refuses the plan's own author, and still outranks administrator rights |
| **Driver departure** | A **Driver** departs their own trip. The cascade that moves shipments and demands into execution runs on the system's behalf, and the driver gains no access to shipments, demands, plans or governance |
| **Loading Tasks** | Releasing a trip creates one loading task per shipment on board. Starting the first moves the trip to **Loading**; settling the last makes it **Ready to Depart**, and an open task holds the vehicle at the gate |
| **Trip lifecycle buttons** | The dispatcher's full trip form is served from `SCOP → Operations → Trips` again. The driver's simplified form is reached only from `My Work → My Trips` |
| **Group By ▸ Status** | Grouping demands, shipments, plans, trips and exceptions by status works, and shows empty lifecycle columns in lifecycle order |
| **Plan Coverage** | Full coverage displays as `100%` |
| **Demand and shipment lifecycles** | A plan release moves its demand to **Released**; departure moves demand and shipment into **In Execution**; the last stop's outcome closes the trip, the shipment and the demand |
| **OTIF** | A completed delivery appears in OTIF. A delivery date captured from a sale order now counts as a commitment — previously only branch opening hours did, so the promise made to the customer was measured against nothing |

:::warning[One item is not repaired retroactively]

Records left mid-flight by Release 1 — a demand still showing **Eligible for Planning** for a trip that has already run — are **not** rewritten by the upgrade. Their history is what it is, and inventing transitions for them would put states in the audit trail that nobody performed.

Expect a pilot database upgraded from Release 1 to show those older operations as incomplete, and to measure OTIF from the first cycle run after the upgrade.

:::

## What is solid today

It is worth being equally precise about what was verified working:

- **Demand is created automatically** from an in-scope Odoo transfer, with nobody typing anything.
- **Ownership is never guessed.** `BR-INV-002` blocks planning and names the exact lines.
- **Readiness is computed from evidence** and carries its reasoning in words, across all five states.
- **Staging changes readiness** immediately and correctly.
- **Planning runs are replayable** — request, response and timing are kept.
- **Separation of duties works**, and outranks administrator rights.
- **An approved plan is frozen**, and modification requires a recorded reason.
- **Actual quantities flow back from the validated Odoo transfer** onto the stop and the demand, exactly as the design intends.
- **`BR-EXE-001` holds**, including for partial deliveries, and asks for the reason at the moment the driver can answer.
- **Proof of delivery** captures signature, photographs and coordinates.
- **A SCOP screen never needs rights over the model a demand came from.** The source order's number is captured on the demand when it is generated, so a planner reads it without any Sales permission — and gains none.
- **A consolidated shipment is one physical visit.** Requirements that share a node, an activity, a date and a committed window become one stop, carrying lines traceable to every demand and every Odoo transfer behind them.
- **The whole cycle runs on operational roles.** Customer Service, Planner, Warehouse, Operations Manager and Driver complete one operating day between them, with no administrator at any step.
- **Loading is measured.** Actual loading duration is recorded per shipment, and an unfinished loading task keeps the vehicle at the gate.
- **The chain closes itself.** Plan release, departure and the last stop's outcome carry the trip, its shipments and its demands to completion without anyone pushing a state by hand.
